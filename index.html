01 / How we work

One system.
Three useful
surfaces.

Start with the work, not the feature list. Every Biogly product begins with a real operating constraint: a decision that takes too long, a handoff that loses context, or a team that cannot see what matters next. We design the smallest useful surface around that constraint.

  1. 01

    Start with the work, not the feature list.

    We sit with the team for a week and write down what actually happens: the questions people ask, the moments they hesitate, the work that gets redone. The constraint is named before any screen is drawn.

  2. 02

    Remove decisions that software should not ask a person to make.

    If a rule can be written down, the product applies it. People keep the judgement calls and lose the sorting, copying and status-chasing that surrounds them.

  3. 03

    Make the next action visible.

    Every screen ends somewhere specific: a person, a deadline, a decision. A customer record becomes a daily brief. A meeting becomes a committed next action. A collection of metrics becomes a weekly review.

  4. 04

    Stay close enough to improve the product in the real world.

    We measure whether the work got lighter, not whether the engagement chart moved. If a feature is used once and never again, it comes out in the next release.

04 / A note from the people building it

Built by people who use the tools.

We started Biogly because too much software asks people to work around it. Our standard is simpler: if a product does not make the next decision clearer, it is not finished. Revu is the first product we have taken all the way to that standard, and it is in production today.

That standard is uncomfortable to hold. It means removing features that were interesting to build. It means measuring whether Monday morning got easier, not whether someone opened the app. We think it is the only way to make software that earns its place on a team's desk for years rather than quarters.
